Phishing commonly uses urgency, account suspension claims, credential requests, impersonation, malicious links, and payment pressure.

URL indicators include lookalike domains, excessive subdomains, unusual TLDs, IP-address hosts, URL shorteners, misleading paths, long URLs, and account or login terminology. These are signals rather than proof of maliciousness.

Email indicators include urgent language, generic greetings, password or OTP requests, unexpected invoices, payment requests, gift-card requests, suspicious links, and instructions to bypass normal security procedures.

Defensive verification should use an independently known website, phone number, bookmark, or official application rather than a link or contact method supplied by a suspicious message.

A single heuristic should not determine a verdict. Context, domain ownership, sender authentication, user expectations, and independent verification should be considered together.
